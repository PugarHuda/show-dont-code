Film yourself running a protocol at the bench. Show, Don't Code watches the video, works out every transfer, and writes an Opentrons Flex protocol that passes the official simulator. You check each step against your own footage before anything touches a reagent.

What happens to your video

Four Meta models, each doing the part it is best at. Nothing reaches the robot until the simulator has run it and you have seen it.

  1. 1

    Watch

    Muse Spark 1.3

    Reads the whole video and the protocol sheet lying on the bench, then looks again at each moment liquid is drawn up to tell which tube it came from.

  2. 2

    Track

    SAM 3.1

    Follows the pipette tip through the recording. Click any tube and it follows that tube too, while Muse Spark throws out the frames where it jumped to a look-alike cap.

  3. 3

    Listen

    Muse Voice Transcribe

    Anything you say while you work, like “fifty microlitres of primer”, becomes timestamped evidence, biased toward the reagent names on your sheet.

  4. 4

    Check

    Muse Spark Open

    A reviewer agent watches the same video, runs the Opentrons simulator itself until the volumes add up, and asks you about whatever the footage can't prove.

The output is code a robot runs

The models decide what happened. Plain, deterministic code decides how the robot does it, so model output never becomes executable code.

# Generated by Show, Don't Code from a video of a human performing the protocol.
requirements = {'robotType': 'Flex', 'apiLevel': '2.20'}

def run(ctx):
    reagents = ctx.load_labware('opentrons_24_tuberack_eppendorf_1.5ml_safelock_snapcap', 'C2')
    tc = ctx.load_module('thermocyclerModuleV2')
    dest = tc.load_labware('opentrons_96_wellplate_200ul_pcr_full_skirt')
    p50 = ctx.load_instrument('flex_1channel_50', 'left', tip_racks=[tips50])
    # step 1 [31-35s]: Transfer MilliQ to tube 1
    p50.transfer(7.75, reagents['A1'], dest['A1'], new_tip='always')
    # step 2 [35-39s]: Transfer MilliQ to tube 2
    p50.transfer(7.75, reagents['A1'], dest['B1'], new_tip='always')
    # step 5 [59-72s]: Transfer SE primer to tube 1
    p50.transfer(1.25, reagents['B1'], dest['A1'], new_tip='always')
    # …
    tc.execute_profile(steps=[…], repetitions=35, block_max_volume=50)
  • Passes the official simulatorThe pcr_3 protocol runs to the end in Opentrons' own simulator: 96 robot actions, one fresh tip per transfer.
  • Every line points back to the videoEach step carries the seconds it happened in your footage, so you can replay the moment before you trust it.
  • Hand-offs where a robot can't helpSpinning down or inverting a tube becomes a pause that hands the run back to you.

On PCR, about one proposed step in four is extra or mislabelled; on a protocol it was never developed on, most reagents come out unnamed. That is why the app shows every step next to the footage, lets you name a tube with one click, and asks before it writes code.

Where it still needs you

A camera can't see everything a protocol needs. The app asks once, in plain words, instead of guessing.

Volumes nobody said out loudOne question per reagent, pre-filled with a plan the reviewer agent checked in the simulator. You decide.
Anything under 1 µLBelow the Flex pipette's minimum, so it asks you to dilute the stock and enter the new volume.
The thermocycler programThe video shows the tubes going in, not the program, so you type the cycle count.
Tubes that look alikeClick a tube to name it once. SAM 3.1 follows it and Muse Spark checks every sighting is really that tube.
